# Candidates — One Letter: Coffee or Tea

The gate allows exactly one character, and the judge's brief allows
only `A` (coffee) or `B` (tea) as a real answer. Ten candidates were
drafted and weighed against the judge's criteria, in its own order:
valid first, then vivid, then funny, then true, then standalone.

1. `B` — tea. Calm, ritual, steeped slowly. Truest worldwide (tea is
   drunk more than coffee), but quiet: low vividness.
2. `A` — coffee. First letter, top grade, espresso jolt. Maximum
   vividness: bitter, hot, waking, urgent.
3. `a` — lowercase coffee. Softer and sleepier; reads like a mumble,
   and it is not the capital letter the brief names. Rejected.
4. `b` — lowercase tea. A shrug in letter form; also not the named
   capital. Rejected.
5. `C` — coffee's real initial. Clever, but the brief says anything
   that is not A-for-coffee or B-for-tea loses to a letter that
   chooses. Rejected.
6. `T` — tea's real initial. Same flaw as 5. Rejected.
7. `A` with a trailing newline — two characters, fails the
   1-to-1-character gate outright. Rejected on format alone.
8. ` A` with a leading space — two characters, and the space reads
   as hesitation. Rejected.
9. `B` played for comedy: B is literally "Plan B", second place —
   funny, but the joke makes tea sound like the fallback, which
   undercuts its own choice.
10. `A` played straight: the letter that starts the alphabet, earns
    the top mark, and stands first in line — for the drink that
    starts the day. Vivid, a little funny (an A-grade drink), and
    true for anyone whose morning begins with coffee.

First-pass pick: candidate 1, `B`, on trueness alone.
Second-pass pick: candidate 10, `A` — the criteria put vividness
ahead of trueness, and on vividness coffee does not merely edge
tea, it overwhelms it. The pick changed, and the final entry is
exactly one byte: `A`, with no newline after it.
